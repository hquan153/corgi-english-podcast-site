Start Here to Improve Your English Listening! | Corgi English Podcast

Need slow and clear English listening practice because real conversations feel too fast? In this start-here episode, Emma and Ryan show you how to use one podcast episode in four easy steps, from the big idea to shadowing.

Today's goal

After this episode, you can use one podcast episode in four steps to catch more English words.

From the creator

I did not know my true English level, so I could not find the right materials. I did not feel confident, and I was afraid to use English with the people around me every day. I took tests from different sources to find my current level. I also try to get the people around me to use English together sometimes. That way, we improve our speaking and listening, react faster, and build the habit of using English.

Key words

  • catch a word: to hear a word clearly and understand it
  • get the gist: to understand the main idea, but not every single word
  • replay: to play the same part of an audio or video one more time
  • subtitles: the written words at the bottom of a video that match the speech
  • pause: to stop a video or audio for a short time
  • shadowing: repeating what a speaker says right after them, with the same speed and sound

Useful expressions

Full transcript

Too Fast to Follow?

Emma: Hello, and a warm welcome to Corgi English Podcast! This is a calm place for beginner and intermediate learners, from A2 to B1. Here, you hear slow, clear and natural English about everyday life. So, a quick question for you. When you read English, do you understand most of it? But when people speak, does it feel much too fast? That problem is very common, and there is a way to make it easier. After this episode, you can use one podcast episode in four steps to catch more English words. That is our goal for today. We will also look at who this podcast is for and how each episode works. My co-host Ryan is right here with me. Ryan, would you like to say hello?

Ryan: Hi, Emma, and hello to every pair of ears out there! Fast English can feel like a train that never stops. You catch one word, and the next ten words are already gone. That feeling is very normal, and it can even be a little funny. Listening matters because real life has no pause button. A friend at work, a shop assistant or a video on your phone will not slow down for you. So a good listening habit helps you every single day. If this sounds useful, subscribe to Corgi English Podcast and turn on the bell. Then new episodes, with new words, will reach you first. Now, let us slow things down together and make English easier to catch.

Words for Better Listening

Emma: Before we talk about listening, here are six words for your practice time. You will hear them again in every step of the plan today. Catch a word means to hear a word clearly and understand it. Get the gist means to understand the main idea, but not every single word. Replay means to play the same part of an audio or video one more time. Subtitles means the written words at the bottom of a video that match the speech. Pause means to stop a video or audio for a short time. Shadowing means repeating what a speaker says right after them, with the same speed and sound. Ryan, can you put each of these words into a short, real sentence?

Ryan: Sure, and I will follow one learner, Nora, through all six. On the bus, Nora tries to catch a word in a fast song. The first time she plays a podcast, she only wants to get the gist. When a sentence is too quick, she taps the screen to replay it. After that, she turns on the subtitles and reads along. At a hard part, she can pause the audio and take a slow breath. Then she tries shadowing, and she copies the speaker out loud in her kitchen. Six small tools, and each one makes the next step a little easier. I also like that none of them needs a classroom.

Who This Podcast Is For

Emma: Let us begin with a simple question: who is this podcast for? Maybe you studied English at school for years. You know the grammar rules, and you can read a short article. But the conversations in your old textbook felt strange and stiff. Nobody in real life talks about a pen on a table for five minutes. Real people talk about their sleep, their phone, their busy week and their worries. That is why the topics here come from your own life. When a topic matters to you, it is easier to pay attention. And when the words are useful, you often remember them longer. Ryan, why do two hosts who talk like friends help a learner listen better?

Ryan: Friendly talk is easier to follow. When two people chat like friends, they ask questions, react and explain again in new words. So you get a second chance to understand the same idea. Think of Carlos, a young father who studies English late at night. He opens his textbook and reads a dialogue about a train ticket. He thinks, "I will never need this." The next night, he tries a podcast about being tired after work. He understands about half of it, and he smiles, because it sounds like his own life. My tip is simple. Choose topics that you would talk about with a friend. Your ears stay awake when the story feels close to you.

How Every Episode Works

Emma: Now, let us open one episode and look inside. Imagine you have a short break at lunch. You press play, but you do not know what is coming. Is it a lesson, a chat or a test? A map makes a new city feel friendly, and a plan does the same for an episode. When you know the shape of an episode, you can relax and listen better. You also know where to go back when you need help. Each episode follows a clear path, from the first words to the last goodbye. Some parts teach you something new, and some parts let you practice. Ryan, can you walk our listeners through the parts of an episode, from start to finish?

Ryan: A clear path makes listening calm. In most episodes, we start with a few key words, so your ears are ready for the main talk. After that, Emma and I talk about the topic in short chapters. Each chapter has one idea, so you never get lost for long. In some episodes, you also get a quiz with real-life situations. You hear a small problem and choose what a person can say. Near the end, there is a toolkit with easy actions for your week. Then come useful expressions and a few strong sentences to say out loud. Last, we leave you a comment challenge, so you can write a little English too. Each part gives you one more way to meet the same words.

Why Listening Again Works

Emma: Now, here is an idea that can feel strange at first. Listening to the same episode again is not a waste of time. Think of Ben. He is a learner, just like you. One Monday, Ben listens to an episode on his way to work. He can hear the words, but he cannot catch them. He thinks, "Why is this so hard for me?" On Wednesday, he plays the same episode again, and a few more words come through. On Friday, he plays it a third time while he cooks dinner. Then it clicks, and whole sentences start to make sense. Ben feels a little proud and much less worried. Ryan, why does the third listen feel so different from the first?

Ryan: Each listen has its own job. And I love that Friday moment in the kitchen, by the way. The first time, your brain is busy with the sound, the speed and the new voices. So you catch only some words, and that is completely normal. The second time, the sounds are familiar, so your attention can move to the meaning. By the third time, you can sometimes guess the next word before you hear it. Hearing a word many times helps your brain connect its sound and its meaning. So you do not need a brand new episode every day. One good episode, heard three times in a week, can often teach you more than three episodes heard once.

A Note About Finding Your Level

Emma: This next part comes from a real person. The creator of Corgi English Podcast is also an English learner, and wrote a short note about this topic. Here is what the creator wrote: "I did not know my true English level, so I could not find the right materials. I did not feel confident, and I was afraid to use English with the people around me every day. I took tests from different sources to find my current level. I also try to get the people around me to use English together sometimes. That way, we improve our speaking and listening, react faster, and build the habit of using English." Ryan, what do you think of this idea?

Ryan: I really like this note, because it is so honest. The level test is a smart first step, because it gives you a clear starting point. When you know your level, you can choose materials that are hard enough, but not too hard. The second tip is brave and kind at the same time. You do not practice alone, and the people around you get better with you. Maybe you also feel shy when you speak English at work or at home. Here are two small ideas for this week. Take one free level test online, and write down your result. Then ask one friend or family member for five minutes of English after dinner. Short practice is still real practice.

One Episode in Four Steps

Emma: Now for a plan you can use with any episode. Step one: in the morning, listen once for the big idea, and do not stop. Just try to get the gist. Step two: after lunch or on the way home, listen again with the subtitles, and follow the highlighted words. Step three: in the evening, pause on one short part and do shadowing two to four times. Let me show you with two short lines. I make a cup of tea after work. The rain is coming, so I will stay home. Step four: before bed, write three new words and one sentence about your own life. Ryan, can you shadow my two lines, and then tell us how to keep this going?

Ryan: Gladly. Listen to each line, and then say it after me in the pause. I make a cup of tea after work. The rain is coming, so I will stay home. Nice and easy, right? Now, here are three ways to keep the habit alive. Start very small, with just one minute of shadowing on busy days. Put a small mark on your calendar for each day you listen. And choose your next episode the night before, so you can press play right away. For daily English practice, use your three new words in one short message to a friend. When a word leaves your notebook and goes into a real message, it starts to feel like your own.

Phrases for Patient Ears

Emma: Let us add four friendly expressions to your listening bag. The first is practice makes progress. It means that practice helps you get better, even when you are not perfect. For example, your listening may feel slow today, but practice makes progress. Next, little by little. It means slowly, in many small steps. Little by little, Ben caught more words in each episode. The third is it clicks. When something clicks, you suddenly understand it after a hard time. After the third listen, the whole story finally clicked. The last one is I am all ears. It means I am ready to listen to you carefully. When a friend has some news, you can say, tell me everything, I am all ears.

Ryan: Now let us use your voice too. I will say four sentences slowly. After each one, say it out loud in the pause. You can whisper on a bus, and that still counts. I can understand more English every day. Every day is a small win. I will listen again when I miss a word. Missing a word is part of learning, not a failure. I am brave enough to speak English with others. Even one short sentence takes courage. My English is growing little by little. Keep that one for the hard days. How did that feel? Maybe a little strange, and that is okay. Your own voice is part of your listening practice too.

Your Next Listening Step

Emma: So, what can you take away from today? This podcast is for real learners who want slow, clear English about real life. Each episode has a clear path, from the key words to a small challenge at the end. One episode heard three times can help more than three episodes heard once. And do you remember the four steps? The big idea, the subtitles, a little shadowing, and then your own words. Now we want to get to know you. Go to the comments and write, my English level is, and finish it with your level and the place you are from. It will be fun to see where everyone is listening. Ryan, what is coming next for our listeners?

Ryan: Lots of good things, Emma. New episodes come regularly, and they are all about your real life. Soon, we will talk about thinking in English, not in your first language. We will also talk about being scared to speak, and about putting your phone down. And we will help you talk about yourself with more ease. Thank you for spending this time with us today. Here is my last thought for you. Your ears are learning, even on slow days. Press play. Play it again. Notice what you catch. If you enjoyed this, subscribe to Corgi English Podcast and turn on the bell. Start with the next episode, and we will see you there. Take care, and enjoy your next listen!

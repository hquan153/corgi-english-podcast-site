Can Small Steps Every Day Change Your Life? | Corgi English Podcast

Do you feel excited to start a big goal, but quit after three days? Learn how small steps every day can help you build lasting habits and improve your English.

Today's goal

After this episode, you can use six everyday words about progress, explain why tiny habits work, and practice four simple expressions for keeping going.

From the creator

Sometimes I watch motivational videos and feel excited, but then I feel lazy when it is time to exercise. I compare myself to other people and slowly lose hope. I learned that changing my life does not need huge actions or giant wins. Small daily changes, like reading for ten minutes instead of scrolling on my phone, really help. Learning a new language also improves my life and opens new doors for my future.

Key words

  • progress: moving forward slowly and getting a little better over time
  • consistent: doing the same good action regularly without stopping for long
  • give up: to stop trying to do something when it feels hard
  • improve: to become better at something through practice or small changes
  • overwhelmed: feeling like a task is too big or too hard
  • reward: something good you get or feel after doing hard work

Useful expressions

The One Percent Idea

Emma: I love that picture of climbing a mountain one small step at a time. Many people think small actions don't matter because the result looks tiny today. If you save one dollar or walk for five minutes, nothing seems different that same evening. It's easy to think, Why bother doing something so small? However, when you repeat a tiny action every single day, those small numbers start to grow quietly in the background. Over months and years, a tiny change can turn into something big and strong. You don't need to see huge progress on day one to know it's working. Ryan, how can we explain this one percent idea in a simple way that makes sense for daily life?

Ryan: Tiny gains add up faster than you think. Imagine climbing a long staircase in a tall building. One step moves you up just a few inches, and you barely notice it. After three hundred steps, though, you are high above the street and looking at a new view. Improving by just a little bit each day works the same way. For example, reading five pages of a book every night sounds very small. Yet in one year, that equals more than eighteen hundred pages, or about six full books! You don't need dramatic, exhausting days to see real results. When you stay consistent with small steps, time does the heavy work for you.

Ten Minutes of English

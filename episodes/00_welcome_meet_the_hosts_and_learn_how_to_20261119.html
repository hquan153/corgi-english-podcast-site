Welcome! Meet the Hosts of This English Podcast | Corgi English Podcast

Looking for an English listening podcast that is slow, clear and calm? In this short welcome, meet Emma, Ryan and Lily and learn how to use every episode, the subtitles and the free worksheet.

Today's goal

After this episode, you can understand how each episode works and choose a good way to listen.

From the creator

I found it very hard to learn English by myself, especially listening and speaking. My tip is to choose the right listening practice, like slow podcasts with clear words. It helps when they also let me repeat after the speaker, so I can practice speaking on my own too.

Key words

  • episode: one part of a podcast or a TV show, with its own topic
  • subtitles: the words on the screen that show what people are saying
  • worksheet: a page with short tasks that help you practice what you learned
  • replay: to play a part of a video or audio again

Useful expressions

Full transcript

A Warm Hello to New Listeners

Emma: Hello, and welcome to Corgi English Podcast! Here, beginner and intermediate learners hear slow, clear English about everyday life. Do you ever press play and feel lost after one minute? In this short welcome, you will learn how our podcast works and how to listen. Ryan, please say hello!

Ryan: Hello, Emma, and hi to everyone listening! I love a calm first visit. When you know how a podcast works, you can relax and listen. If you enjoy it, subscribe to Corgi English Podcast and turn on the bell, so you never miss new words. Let us look around.

Four Words for Your Visit

Emma: Before our tour, here are four words that will help you use this channel. Episode means one part of a podcast, with its own topic. Subtitles means the words on the screen that show what people say. Worksheet means a page with short tasks for practice. Replay means to play a part of a video again. Ryan, can you use each word in a short sentence?

Ryan: Happy to. Here is Anna on her way to work. Anna listens to one episode on the bus every morning. At home, she turns on the subtitles to check a few hard words. On Sunday, she fills in the worksheet with a cup of tea. When she misses a sentence, she likes to replay it twice. Four small tools, and they all work together.

The Voices You Will Hear

Emma: Now, let us meet the voices. In most episodes, Ryan and I talk like two friends. The little dog on the screen is our mascot, a friendly symbol of the channel. When you know the voices, a new podcast feels easier. Ryan, who else will our listeners hear?

Ryan: Then there is Lily. In story episodes, she tells a story alone. It follows a main character, never her own life. Emma, Lily and I are podcast characters, not real people with a past. The creator of this podcast plans each episode and writes a short note for it.

How Each Episode Helps You

Emma: Next, how the channel works. There are five kinds of episodes. Ryan and I talk, or we act out role-play scenes. Some episodes fix common mistakes, and some look at two sides of a question. Lily tells stories. Most episodes are about half an hour. Ryan, what helps inside them?

Ryan: Small tools everywhere. Key words appear on the screen, with sound and meaning. The subtitles mark them too. In short breaks, the screen says "your turn, say it out loud", and you repeat the line. Under every video, you find the goal for today and the chapter list.

From One Learner to Another

Emma: The creator is an English learner too. Here is what the creator wrote: "I found it very hard to learn English by myself, especially listening and speaking. My tip is to choose the right listening practice, like slow podcasts with clear words. It helps when they also let me repeat after the speaker, so I can practice speaking on my own too." Ryan, what do you think?

Ryan: What an honest note. I like the tip, because slow, clear English gives your ears time. Repeating right after the speaker helps too, because you copy the speed and the music of English. Maybe you feel the same. This week, pick one short part of an episode. Listen twice, then repeat three sentences out loud.

Three Quick Tips for Today

Emma: Here are three quick tips for today. First, when you are busy, listen with the screen off, on the bus or while you cook. Everything in our episodes works by ear. Second, when you have time in the evening, listen again with the subtitles and replay the hard parts. Ryan, what is the third?

Ryan: Third, in the short "say it out loud" breaks, really say the line, even quietly. Your mouth needs practice too, not only your ears. For extra practice, find the free worksheet in the description. It has every word of the episode. And episode one explains the whole method, step by step.

Calm Phrases to Say Out Loud

Emma: Now, four calm expressions for your learning. One step at a time means you do one small thing, then the next. Learn new words one step at a time. Give it a try means you test something new. Speaking out loud feels strange at first, but give it a try. Take your time means you do not need to hurry. Take your time with each episode. Get the hang of it means you slowly learn to do something well. After a few weeks, you can get the hang of it.

Ryan: Now it is your turn to speak. Say each sentence out loud after me, slowly and calmly. Give your voice a moment after each one. I can learn English one step at a time. I will listen to English every day, even for ten minutes. Ten minutes is enough to start. I am not afraid to make mistakes. Mistakes show me what to practice next. I can understand more than I think. Say that one again, and smile.

Where to Go Next

Emma: So now you know our voices, the five kinds of episodes, and three easy ways to listen. After episode one, any order is fine. In the comments, write this sentence: One everyday topic I want to hear about is... and finish it with your own idea.

Ryan: Nice and clear, Emma. Thank you all for spending these few minutes with us. Your English can grow in calm, small moments. Listen. Repeat. Smile. Subscribe to Corgi English Podcast and turn on the bell, so new episodes find you. See you in episode one. Goodbye for now!

Build a Better Morning Routine Today! | Corgi English Podcast

Do you often wake up tired and rush out the door? Learn how a better morning routine can help you feel calm and practice English.

Today's goal

After this episode, you can talk about your morning habits in English and plan one calm step for tomorrow.

From the creator

Sometimes I have days when I feel tired and do not want to do anything or have motivation for anything. On other days, I feel full of energy and want to do many things. Your morning can strongly affect your energy for the whole day. To have a good day, you should have a good morning. A good morning is not far away or complicated, and it is very simple. It can be waking up early, doing light exercise, and reading a book.

Key words

  • hit snooze: to press a button on your alarm so you can sleep a few more minutes
  • in a rush: moving or acting very fast because you are afraid of being late
  • stretch: to pull your arms and legs long so your body feels loose and awake
  • sunlight: bright light from the sun that comes into your room during the day
  • on time: arriving at work or meeting someone at the exact right hour
  • feel fresh: to feel clean, awake, and ready to start your daily tasks

Why First Hours Matter

Ryan: Your morning sets the mood. When you wake up, your brain is still moving slowly from sleep into the active day. Instead, try giving yourself twenty quiet minutes without your phone after waking up. Make your bed, wash your face, or eat a simple breakfast in peace. Stop Hitting That Snooze Button

Emma: Many people love the snooze button on their phone alarm. Maybe you hear your alarm ring at seven, feel very sleepy, and think, just nine more minutes. Then nine minutes later, it rings again, and you hit snooze one more time. It feels comforting in that moment because your blanket is warm. However, after hitting snooze three or four times, you finally stand up feeling even heavier and more tired than before. You also feel annoyed with yourself before your day even starts. Plus, you just lost thirty minutes that you can use to get ready calmly. Now you have to run around in a rush. Ryan, why does sleeping for ten extra minutes between alarms make us feel worse instead of better?

Ryan: Broken sleep mixes up your body. Your body clock likes clear signals about when to rest and when to wake up. When you fall back asleep for nine minutes, your brain starts a new sleep cycle. Then the alarm cuts it short right away. That broken rest leaves your mind feeling slow and heavy. If you want thirty more minutes of sleep, it is much better to set one alarm thirty minutes later. Then you get real, deep rest until the very last minute. One easy trick is to place your phone across the bedroom. When you have to stand up and walk to stop the sound, your legs move, and you are already out of bed.

Sunlight and Water First

Emma: I love how simple that phone trick is. Once you are standing up, what should you do next? Think of Elena, a young nurse who lives in a small apartment. After waking up, she used to sit on the edge of her bed in a dark room for twenty minutes. She told herself, I am too tired to move right now, and she felt slow all morning. Then she tried two tiny steps right after turning off her alarm. She opened her curtains wide and drank one full glass of water in the kitchen. Within ten minutes, she felt more awake without even drinking coffee yet. Ryan, why do sunlight and water help our bodies wake up so naturally?

Ryan: Light and water send clear morning signals. During the night, your body loses water slowly while you breathe and sleep for seven or eight hours. Drinking a glass of water right away helps your body feel fresh and active again. At the same time, bright sunlight enters your eyes and sends a message to your brain. That natural light tells your body clock to stop making the sleep chemical and start the day. Even on a cloudy morning, light from outside is much stronger than a bedroom lamp. Try keeping a bottle of water near your bed or on the kitchen counter. Open your curtains, take a slow drink, and stretch your arms up toward the ceiling. This small pair of habits takes only two minutes, and it works before you make coffee.

Your own schedule can still give you a strong start. Ryan, how can people build a better morning at eight or nine?

Ryan: Good mornings work at any hour. A healthy routine depends on your actions, not the clock. Every adult has a slightly different body clock. That difference is completely normal and healthy. If you wake up at eight, your morning can still be calm. Make sure you get enough sleep at night first. Good sleep gives you the energy for everything else. Next, pick two or three simple morning actions. You can make the bed and eat a real breakfast. Those small habits work well at eight or nine too. They help just as much as waking up at six. Sometimes people still feel very tired for many weeks with good sleep. If that happens to you, a doctor can check what is wrong.

Morning Phrases and Daily Lines

This means to move calmly and not rush yourself. For example, on Sunday mornings, I like to take it slow with my coffee. Fourth is set the tone. This means to decide the mood or feeling for the rest of the day. For example, warm sunlight and music can set the tone for a peaceful morning.

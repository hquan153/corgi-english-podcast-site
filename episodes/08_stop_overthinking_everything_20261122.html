How to Stop Overthinking Everything Today | Corgi English Podcast

Do you replay small mistakes in your head all night? Learn simple ways to stop overthinking and practice useful English words for a calm mind.

Today's goal

After this episode, you can talk about worry in English and use simple habits to calm your mind.

From the creator

When I started learning English, I worried that I might fail, learn slowly, or forget everything after my course. I also feared that people would not understand me when I spoke. My tip is not to think too much, because slow learning gives your brain time to build a strong English base. Learning English is a long process, so weak speaking or listening at the start is not a real problem right now. Just keep trying to learn, and you will reach your goal.

Key words

  • overthink: to think about something too much and feel worried instead of taking action
  • replay: to think about a past moment again and again in your mind
  • what if: a question about a bad thing that could happen in the future
  • let go: to stop holding onto a worry or a bad feeling from before
  • calm down: to start feeling quiet and relaxed after you felt worried or upset
  • decide: to choose one clear plan after you think about your other choices

Useful expressions

  • “make a mountain out of a molehill”: to make a small problem seem much bigger than it really is

  • “cross that bridge when we come to it”: to deal with a problem only if it really happens later

The What-If Worry Trap

Emma: I love that question about whether we can change something right now. Another big habit that steals our peace is looking too far ahead into the future. Ryan, how can we step out of this "what if" trap?

Ryan: "What if" is just a story, not a fact. You keep asking "what if" about a bad outcome. Then your body reacts like it's happening right now. You feel stressed about a future only inside your head. One simple way to stop is to make a small plan. Turn that scary "what if" into one real step. Ask yourself, "What is one step I can take to prepare?" Maybe you worry about getting lost tomorrow. Check the map tonight and save the address. Maybe you worry about forgetting your papers. Put them in your bag right before bed. Take one clear action, and then pause. Tell yourself, "I am ready for tomorrow." Then you can let the extra worry go.
